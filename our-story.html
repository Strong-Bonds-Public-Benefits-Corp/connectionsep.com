Our Story

We're not a board. We're survivors who decided to build what we did not have but needed.

Co-Founder

Tony Sorensen

20-year U.S. Army SSG. Three deployments.

“Fort Bliss nearly ended my life. Then Marylou gave me an idea — and building it saved me.”

In November 2023, Tony attempted to end his life.

Twenty years in the Army. Three deployments to Afghanistan, tours in Bahrain and Qatar, a year in USARAF. It wasn't combat that nearly killed him. It was Fort Bliss — the institution he gave everything to.

Before that night, he'd written it all down — everything the Army was getting wrong. A “Words to Live By” note, meant to spotlight the problems. He sent it up the entire chain — the Secretary of the Army, the Sergeant Major of the Army, the Secretary of Defense, the FORSCOM commanding general and his senior enlisted advisor.

He survived the night. In intensive outpatient therapy afterward, he met Marylou. He told her about the coffee shop he'd been dreaming about. She said seven words that changed everything: “Why not tie it to mental health?”

That was her idea. Not his. He asked her to build it with him — because the thing that made it matter, the thing that made it a mission instead of a shop, came from her.

Coffee & Connections exists because in November 2023, Tony needed a place like this. And there wasn't one.

Co-Founder

Marylou Culpepper

U.S. Army Veteran. MST Survivor. Sarah's mom.

“The system that was supposed to protect me is the one I had to survive. I'm an MST survivor, an Army veteran, and Sarah's mom — Sarah's Place carries her name. I lived the injustice. Now I'm building what should have been there when I needed it.”

Marylou joined the Army to serve. Instead she learned what it feels like when the institution built to protect you protects itself.

She is a survivor of Military Sexual Trauma. And the assault wasn't the only wound — it was what came after. How the Army treated her. How it turned its back. She left the Army carrying PTSD and a VA rating because of what was done to her, and because of how the system answered.

She served in Signals Intelligence — where precision is everything — and she brought that same precision to designing the “Survivor-First” standards that run this entire operation. She knows exactly what a system that fails survivors looks like. She lived inside one.

She's Sarah's mom. Sarah's Place, our transitional housing for domestic violence survivors, carries her daughter's name — because Marylou is building the world she wants Sarah to grow up in. One where survivors are believed. Where there's a place to land.

Co-Founder & Chief Operating Officer

Dawn Sorensen

Retail operations, clinical support logistics, and military family advocacy.

Dawn has spent nearly two decades running high-volume coffee retail and clinical support operations. She managed Starbucks locations in San Antonio and at Fort Lewis — including the store inside Madigan Army Medical Center, serving medical staff, injured personnel, and families in crisis — and ran the deli and Starbucks kiosk at Fred Meyer in Oregon through the height of COVID-19. Most recently she worked at Rim Rock Trails, a behavioral health agency in Oregon, and as a Medical Logistics Specialist — the foundation for our clinical-grade hygiene protocols. She is a Certified Food Safety Manager and a Starbucks Coffee Master.

She is a military spouse of 19 years. She held the home front through multiple combat deployments and PCS moves — Texas, Washington, Germany, Oregon — and worked in the DoDEA school system in Germany, the touchpoint for military kids and families during deployment cycles. While Tony navigated PTSD as a soldier, Dawn navigated being the primary support system. She built this place to be the space she wished she had: somewhere spouses and families could find community without the stigma of a clinical waiting room. For a military family, a cup of coffee is often the only moment of normalcy in a chaotic day. She is moving from Oregon to El Paso to run the floor with Tony — the founding crew, side by side, from opening day.

Co-Founder & Chief Technology Officer

Vic Fernandez

Cybersecurity, DevOps, and AI/ML.

Vic is a Chief Warrant Officer 3 in the U.S. Army with 17 years of active duty, currently assigned to the Army AI Integration Center in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. He has managed the cybersecurity program for a cloud platform built to host AI/ML applications, designed security architecture for military networks and weapon systems, and run Microsoft enterprise systems. He holds a master’s in Cybersecurity Studies and a bachelor’s in Information Systems Security from American Military University, and certifications including CISSP and OSCP.

He is building the Sanctuary App and the Strong Bonds Tech platform — the daily check-in, the Resilience Heat Map, and the systems that keep survivor data protected. The tech exists for one reason: so the café can see the community’s resilience in real time and catch people before crisis — without ever collecting a story nobody offered. Vic and his multi-veteran family live with childhood trauma and PTSD themselves. He builds because he knows what a safe space is worth. He is passionate about security automation, detection engineering — and anything paranormal.
