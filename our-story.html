Our Story

We're not a board. We're survivors who decided to build what we did not have but needed.

Co-Founder

Tony Sorensen

20-year U.S. Army SSG. Three deployments.

“Fort Bliss nearly ended my life. Then Marylou gave me an idea — and building it saved me.”

In November 2023, Tony attempted to end his life.

Twenty years in the Army. Three deployments to Afghanistan, tours in Bahrain and Qatar, a year in Africa. It wasn't combat that nearly killed him. It was Fort Bliss — the institution he gave everything to.

Before that night, he'd written it all down — everything the Army was getting wrong. A “Words to Live By” note, meant to spotlight the problems. He sent it up the entire chain — the Secretary of the Army, the Sergeant Major of the Army, the Secretary of Defense, the FORSCOM commanding general and his senior enlisted advisor.

He survived the night. In intensive outpatient therapy afterward, he met Marylou. He told her about the coffee shop he'd been dreaming about. She said seven words that changed everything: “Why not tie it to mental health?”

That was her idea. Not his. He asked her to build it with him — because the thing that made it matter, the thing that made it a mission instead of a shop, came from her.

Coffee & Connections exists because in November 2023, Tony needed a place like this. And there wasn't one.

Co-Founder

Marylou Culpepper

U.S. Army Veteran. MST Survivor. Sarah's mom.

“The system that was supposed to protect me is the one I had to survive. I'm an MST survivor, an Army veteran, and Sarah's mom — Sarah's Place carries her name. I lived the injustice. Now I'm building what should have been there when I needed it.”

Marylou joined the Army to serve. Instead she learned what it feels like when the institution built to protect you protects itself.

She is a survivor of Military Sexual Trauma. And the assault wasn't the only wound — it was what came after. How the Army treated her. How it turned its back. She left the Army carrying PTSD and a VA rating because of what was done to her, and because of how the system answered.

She served in Signals Intelligence — where precision is everything — and she brought that same precision to designing the “Survivor-First” standards that run this entire operation. She knows exactly what a system that fails survivors looks like. She lived inside one.

She's Sarah's mom. Sarah's Place, our transitional housing for domestic violence survivors, carries her daughter's name — because Marylou is building the world she wants Sarah to grow up in. One where survivors are believed. Where there's a place to land.
