How it works

Anonymous means anonymous — and we show our math

Zip areas need 10 or more check-ins to show. Filtered views — one age group or one military connection inside one zip — need 5 or more. Anything below that shows grey: "not enough data yet." We publish these numbers openly so you can trust what you see — and what you don't. A grey area isn't a zero. It's us refusing to guess from too few voices.

What never reaches this map: names, aliases, and emails never enter the pipeline at all. Birth year becomes an age band before aggregation — the raw year never appears here. No address is ever collected; zip code is the only geography we ask for. "Prefer not to say" answers count in the overall view and in no filtered view.

What the lenses mean: picking a group doesn't change the map — it filters it. The same zip areas re-color using only that group's check-ins. That's how leaders can see, for example, how veterans in one neighborhood are doing compared with everyone in that neighborhood.

County view is coming. County isn't collected on the check-in; it will be derived from zip codes with a standard lookup when the real pipeline is built, and the map will gain a Zip | County toggle then.

For leaders: this map shows trends, not people. It can't diagnose anyone, identify anyone, or move resources by itself — but it can show a commander, a superintendent, or a council member where support is needed early, before crisis. That's the point: catching people before crisis, not after.
